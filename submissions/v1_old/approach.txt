I fine-tune google/byt5-small (pinned revision) on train.csv and use it as a word-by-word analyser. The encoder reads the whole hieroglyphic sentence as raw UTF-8 bytes, so every sign and every <g> code is read as written and no vocabulary or tokenizer is fitted on any file. Because the word boundaries are given, the decoder runs once per word: it cross-attends only to the encoder states of that word's bytes, which already carry the sentence context, and writes transliteration;lemma;UPOS;gloss for it. The four layers of a word are generated together by the same fine-tuned model, and the output has exactly one analysis per hieroglyphic word by construction, so nothing drifts in long sentences.

Training is 12 epochs of AdamW with warmup and linear decay, bf16 autocast, and batches of a fixed size. Decoding is greedy. A small cleaning step only fixes the shape of a group (four fields, no spaces) and blanks the longest groups if a row would pass the length cap.

I validated on the latest training sentences (19th century BCE and later held out, the rest used for fitting), with separate figures per layer and for seen and unseen word spellings, then fitted the submitted model on all of train.csv. No dictionary, sign list, lookup table or external text is used, and each test sentence is analysed independently; batching is only for speed. Larger batches with half as many updates were tried and dropped: they scored lower on the held-out sentences.
